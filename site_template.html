Should recess be outside tomorrow?

A next-day PM2.5 forecast for Almaty, built for the person who has to decide each afternoon whether tomorrow's PE lesson and recess happen outdoors.

Who it's for

PE

A school duty officer or PE teacher in Almaty

Almaty sits in a bowl under the Zailiysky Alatau. Every heating season (October–April) coal smoke from the private-housing districts gets trapped under cold-air inversions, and evening PM2.5 spikes. Children breathe faster than adults and count as a sensitive group, so on bad days outdoor exercise should move inside.

The decision is made the day before, around 3 PM, when the next day's timetable is set. That's when this tool runs: python predict.py → indoors / outdoors-light / outdoors.

Results on data the model never saw

The decision: smog days caught vs. false alarms

A "smog day" is a daily mean above 35.4 µg/m³ (the US EPA level where air becomes unhealthy for sensitive groups). A missed smog day means children exercise in bad air, and a false alarm costs one outdoor lesson. We counted a miss as 3× worse than a false alarm and chose the warning cut-off on a validation season (Oct 2024 – Sep 2025), never on the test data.

Where it fails

Why.The largest misses are sudden changes: a snow or rain front that washes the air clean overnight (1 Jan 2026), or a sharp jump after a holiday (New Year's Eve: fireworks and heating). The model leans on today's evening PM2.5, so it reacts a day late to sudden breaks. More weather detail (inversion strength, mixing height) would help.

What drives the forecast

Honest limits

PM2.5 values are model estimates, not station measurements. They come from Open-Meteo's air quality API (Copernicus CAMS, about 11 km grid). Ground stations in Almaty's lower districts often read far higher on winter evenings, so the absolute levels are probably underestimated.

"Tomorrow's weather" in training is the archived forecast/analysis, which is slightly more accurate than a real day-ahead forecast. Live use relies on a real forecast, so expect slightly larger errors.

This project is a prototype and is not medical advice. Schools should follow official guidance from Kazhydromet and health authorities.
